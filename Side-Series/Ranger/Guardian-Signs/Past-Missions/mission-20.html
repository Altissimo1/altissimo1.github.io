Level Strategy

You will only get 3 minutes when beginning the level, but there are a lot of time-upgrades to pick up across the level. Start by collecting all four time extensions (a total of 2 minutes) in the starting area, then take the teleporter.

  • First room: Espeon or Shelgon guard the yellow switches to advance in this room, and may need to be captured to give you enough time to stand on the switches to wait for them to reveal their paths. Neither Pokémon is hard to capture, but time is tight. When stepping on the third platform, move diagonally to avoid a Shelgon or Espeon that rushes you. Before taking the teleporter, go down the left and right patchs to pick up two 90-second time powerups.
  • Second room: Several chests along the right side contain powerups, but the final 60-second powerup is guarded by an Absol or Houndoom, which is very difficult to capture. Heading west, either you will find Honchkrow guarded by five Murkrow on the same platform as the Houndoom/Absol, or on the platform below; either way, collect all the Murkrow, do your best to avoid the Honchkrow, and pick up additional powerups. Go southeast to get a Power powerup, and then southwest to the teleporter.
  • Third room: Go west to pick up a speed powerup and a time powerup, as well as a Delibird in Pattern 3. Go east for the last Murkrow, which you will have to chase; use diagonals to intercept its square-shaped path. You will almost certainly need the speed powerup for this. Head south, avoiding the Pokémon guarding the area.
  • Fourth room: Do your best to avoid the Houndoom or Absol, and the Honchkrow that launch twisters instead of chasing you. Staying on the outer edge of the platform helps avoid these encounters. Go right instead of left, taking the bottom-right teleporter to a room with some additional powerups, and then after returning, go south toward the red warp.
Pattern Identification

The available Pokémon depend on the pattern.

  • The Pokémon surrounding the starting area are Houndoom: Pattern 1 or 2.
  • The Pokémon surrounding the starting area are Absol: Pattern 3 or 4.
  • On an Absol pattern, the Pokémon in the second room are Espeon: Pattern 3. There is a Delibird in this pattern if you want to AP grind.
  • On an Absol pattern, the Pokémon in the second room are Shelgon: Pattern 4.
  • On a Houndoom pattern, the Murkrow guarding the Honchkrow are on the first platform in a ( shape: Pattern 1.
  • On a Houndoom pattern, the Murkrow guarding the Honchkrow are on the second platform in a ) shape: Pattern 2.
Minibosses

Both Houndoom and Absol deal heavy damage, appear multiple times, and chase you relentlessly, so you can run out of energy early on. Houndoom also agitates at about 60% gauge loss.

Houndoom's attacks are:

  • Flamethrower: A directional barrage of small flames. 10 damage per hit.
  • Dark Pulse: Creates circular waves in random parts of the screen that fade extremely quickly but overlap to cover a huge area. Unpredictable, though the screen corners seem safer. 8 damage per hit.
  • Inferno: An arcing fireball erupting into a column of fire lasting ~9 seconds. Houndoom stays still for a couple seconds while launching it, a good window to loop it quickly. 8 damage per hit.

Absol's attacks are:

  • Dark blade arc: A flurry of blades in a semicircle. 10 damage per hit.
  • Converging blades: The same arc as the dark blade arc, but slower, and the blades converge back to Absol after traveling halfway across the screen.
  • Dark Pulse: Identical to Houndoom's 8 damage per hit.

Honchkrow is less annoying, but it can be difficult to avoid when capturing Murkrow, and it still isn't exactly easy.

  • Dark blades: 3 blades that move at a medium speed in straight lines away from Honchkrow.
  • Dark Pulse (!! and !!!): Identical to Houndoom's.

Boss

  • Cutting attack (!): A cut attack directly in front of it, often accompanied by the (!!!!) dark blades. 10 damage per hit. Difficult to loop around.
  • Split the ground (!!): Raises 6 rock pillars close around itself. 8 damage per hit. Possible to loop around.
  • Split the ground (!!!): Raises rock pillars around itself in a different formation. 8 damage per hit. Impossible to loop around.
  • Scatters dark blades (!!!!): Launches 8 dark blades behind it, which loop back to Gliscor's front like boomerang. This is often used untelegraphed alongside other attacks. 10 damage per hit. Possible to loop around.

S-Rank Tips

Gliscor is a real challenge, but the biggest pain is Houndoom and Absol, which case waste significant amounts of time and energy. Hug the walls and use diagonal movement to avoid encounters as best you can. If you are reasonably good at dodging, A rank is likely; if you are very good at dodging, S rank can be done early.

Partner Choice

Piplup is still a good choice due to its type matchup, despite its low damage. One suggestion for how best to use Piplup (or any short-range attacker with a short attack time), suggested by Punchshroom on the post below the one linked above, is to place Piplup immediately behind Gliscor at the earliest possible moment after it uses its dark blade attack. The dark blades will not pass over Piplup's position, so it will not be knocked out, and Gliscor will not move until the blades clear the screen, meaning the Assist is guaranteed to hit from here. See the pictures in Punchshroom's post for more information.

Gengar also works very well when placed in the same spot, since its Pause effect can guard it from any follow-up attacks from Gliscor as well. Froslass has a lot of damage, but it can be difficult to time its Assist; Punchshroom recommends using a level 4 Froslass instead of level 5. If other Assists are used, consider the positioning trick mentioned above and prioritize high-damage, low-time Assists.
