Level Strategy

This level has a large and winding layout with a single "correct path": wrong teleporters cost time. For grinding, Magmar and Charmeleon (each worth 3 AP) are worth capturing despite being challenging. Their attacks target fixed screen positions, rather than following the player, so time your movements around them carefully, especially in the final room. Skip Charmander and Cyndaquil unless you want thier Slates.

Pattern Identification

In this Mission, two of the teleporter spaces lead to different areas of the map in different patterns. Additionally, Flareon and different Tyrogue evolutions are also specific to different patterns. Unfortunately, however, one of the Magby's locations is dependent on the pattern, but in such a way that the player will not be able to identify which pattern they are on before finding the Magby. The player therefore has a 50% chance of taking the wrong turn and going to a dead-end with a single powerup. At that point, however, the pattern will be identified.

  • First Pokémon encountered is Cyndaquil, and Magby is at the end of the top path in the same chamber: Pattern 1.. In this pattern, in the chamber with the two-player switches and platform, the top-right teleporter leads to a dead end with power-ups whereas the top-left teleporter leads to progress.
  • First Pokémon encountered is Cyndaquil, and Magby is not at the end of the top path in the same chamber: Pattern 3. In this pattern, in the chamber with the two-player switches and platform, the top-right teleporter leads to a dead end with power-ups whereas the top-left teleporter leads to progress.
  • First Pokémon encountered is Charmander: Pattern 2 or 4. Identifying which pattern the player is on after this determines whether it is the top-left or top-right teleporter in the chamber with the two-player switches and platform that leads to progress.
  • On a Charmander pattern, the chamber with the two-player switches and platform has a Magby in the bottom right: Pattern 4. In this pattern, the top-right teleporter leads to a dead end with power-ups whereas the top-left teleporter leads to progress. Flareon can only be found in this pattern.
  • On a Charmander pattern, the chamber with the two-player switches and platform does not have a Magby in the bottom right: Pattern 2. In this pattern, the top-left teleporter leads to a dead end with power-ups whereas the top-right teleporter leads to progress (different from the other patterns).

Boss

  • Scatters fire (!): A single line of fire in the direction it is facing. Travels to the screen edge. 4 damage per hit. Impossible to loop around.
  • Spews flames (!!): A larger single line of fire in the direction it is facing. Does not travel to the screen edge. 5 damage per hit. Impossible to loop around.
  • Explosion (!!!): Only used while agitated. Will be used with no telegraphing as it runs around the screen, and will combo into other attacks. 6 damage per hit. Impossible to loop around unless it can be slowed with a Poké Assist.
  • Flame pillars (!!!!): Two fire pillars in a V shape in the direction it is facing. 3 damage per hit. Impossible to loop around.

S-Rank Tips

Not feasible yet. Return much later once you can capture Bulbasaur in 3-4 loops, navigate past angry Pineco/Carnivine without engaging them, and have a strong Poké Assist with good range (at level 24, Blaziken worked well).

Partner Choice

Close-range attacks can be good against Magmortar's single-line attacks, but do not help much against the explosions. Poké Assists with quick recovery time are important here for preventing Magmortar's gauge from depleting significantly as it uses its impossible-to-loop attacks. A Poké Assist that can slow it down makes quick loops during the explosion attack possible.

Piplup remains excellent for its type advantage, long range, and slowing effect. Despite Sandslash's type advantage, its Assist's pattern doesn't suit Magmortar's frequent movement.
